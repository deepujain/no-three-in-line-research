# Proposed bounded checkpoint continuation runner

Design only. This is not implemented or approved, and it does not start a numerical search. The aim is to start the next explicitly authorized checkpoint slice promptly after accepting the previous one, without changing the reviewed DFS, checkpoint core, owned-process helper or guard. Review the finished runner and exact source hashes before any use.

## Bounded invocation and resource accounting

Run one checkpoint invocation at a time in the chain process by calling the approved checkpoint guard's `run` directly. The guard retains ownership of the worker and its own SIGTERM callback; do not nest the existing helper around a guard process that itself starts a new session. Pass each invocation at most 30 seconds and 256 MiB sampled RSS, with a positive fixed node cap supplied by the allocation. A node cap and time limit bound an invocation, not the entire mathematical slice. A separately enforced supervisor deadline and aggregate reserved budget cover the complete chain process, not merely its numerical workers.

Use a maximum chain reservation of 900 seconds, including setup, checking, publication, waits and cleanup. Conservatively allocate at most 26 invocations; before launching each, reserve 32 seconds (30 for its guard plus two for orchestration), record the reservation, and require at least 32 seconds before an internal 840-second launch cutoff. Stop launching at the cutoff and retain the last 60 seconds for cleanup and terminal accounting. Record both elapsed wall time and reserved total; never reclaim a reservation based on guessed or missing receipts. This is an operational planning bound, not a promise that filesystem calls, cleanup, or a hard-killed process finish within the budget. If the outer supervisor kills the chain or a child cannot be confirmed reaped, record an incomplete/unknown outcome and do not start a replacement automatically.

## Identity, ownership and accepted transitions

Freeze and record hashes for the chain runner, checkpoint guard and core, fast and original cores, geometry verifier, owned-process helper, and actual dispatcher; bind n, exact sorted deficit/diagonal selections, orbit-order and branch-order identity, node cap, time/memory gates and maximum reservation. Before each launch and after it returns, verify the same hashes and input identity. The first expected checkpoint SHA must come from the Lead's independently verified receipt, not by hashing an arbitrary supplied checkpoint and treating it as trusted. Thereafter, the sole next expected SHA comes from the immediately preceding accepted invocation after the checks below. The dispatcher is report-bound rather than part of the checkpoint core identity; explicitly compare it on every transition.

Take a nonblocking chain-specific lock before reading state and retain it through the final ledger write. Each called guard still takes its existing checkpoint lock. A distinct chain lock avoids deadlocking against the guard's checkpoint flock; all participating chain runners must honor it. A standalone uncooperative writer can bypass the chain lock, so each guard's pinned SHA check remains mandatory and a mismatch stops the chain. No reset, deletion, archive, overwrite of an unrelated checkpoint, or automatic adoption of unexpected bytes is permitted.

Before each launch, atomically publish an intent record containing sequence number, expected digest, unique report path, full job arguments, source hashes and reservation. Invoke the guard with the accepted checkpoint and a fresh report path. Only after the worker returns, verify a successful guard exit, clean owned-process cleanup, source stability, positive sampled RSS within the cap, elapsed deadline, exact report and checkpoint SHA, predecessor digest, n/slice/source/order identity, and monotone counter delta no larger than the node cap. Check that the report is accepted and its terminal status is one of `node_limit`, `timeout`, or `all_declared_slices_exhausted`; never infer acceptance from process exit zero alone. Independently validate every saved witness with both geometry checkers, correct point count and reflection symmetry; retain all witness files and stop for review on any witness. Atomically record the accepted report, accepted frontier digest, counters, witness results, elapsed time and remaining reservation before launching another invocation. Mark complete only when the accepted frontier explicitly exhausts every declared slice; this remains a family-restricted conclusion.

Stop on a resource, monitor, cleanup, source, identity, hash, lock, validation or cancellation failure; an exhausted accepted frontier; a witness; exhausted reservation; or incomplete/missing receipt. The per-invocation cooperative `timeout` may be accepted as an incomplete checkpoint only when the external guard and every validation gate are clean; make whether to continue such timeouts an explicit allocation policy. Default conservatively to stopping after timeout so an unexpectedly costly node or publication does not repeat indefinitely. A normal node-limit checkpoint can advance automatically only inside the authorized bounds.

## Interruption and recovery

Install the chain's SIGTERM handler outside guard calls, set a cancellation flag and stop before any subsequent launch. During a guard call, the guard's handler owns cancellation and must return a cancelled or cleanup-error report; after it restores the chain handler, persist the result and stop. Treat SIGKILL, missing final reports, failed report writes and unverifiable process cleanup as unknown, not completed. If a crash occurs before checkpoint promotion, retry only after explicit review of the recorded intent, trusted old digest and ownership state. If a crash or rejection occurs after promotion but before accepted ledger publication, retain the incomplete intent and distinguish any published-but-unaccepted digest from an accepted digest. The predecessor field alone cannot authenticate a substituted checkpoint; require a deliberate trusted recovery decision. Never automatically resume from a digest recovered solely by hashing the current file.

## Prerequisite verification

Test the actual chain boundary with n=3 and n=5 only: several small node slices exactly match the unsliced witness order and per-slice counts; fail on changed source, selection, trusted digest, checkpoint and malformed receipt; exercise reservation exhaustion, timeout policy, witness stop, concurrent lock contention, SIGTERM between and during guard calls, child/cleanup failure, and interruptions before and after checkpoint and ledger publication. Fault-inject delayed report writes and budget overruns. Verify no overlapping launches and that the existing guard/helper tests still pass. A real-host tiny CLI or process-lifecycle gate must confirm OS monitoring, child ownership and cancellation, followed by independent review of exact frozen runner and combined entrypoint hashes. No n=79 allocation follows from this document alone.
