# 157-point deficiency walk: independent mathematical design review

Decision: PASS as a bounded exploratory design. This review reads the proposal SHA-256 `0213b6bf2ee99ec77c443dc618a7eef74d149f80ae3ca453c1c4e4741f656588`, existing scoring/rectangle helpers and both verifier sources. No implementation, tiny enumeration, n=79 evaluation, search, formula or solver was run. Source/tiny review and an explicit finite proposal cap remain necessary before any separately allocated actual run.

For a set of 2n-1 cells with each row degree at most two, the sum of deficits 2-degree is 1. All deficits are nonnegative integers, so exactly one row has degree one and all others degree two; the same argument gives exactly one deficient column. In particular no row or column can be empty. This proof works for n >= 2 as stated.

Let S(A) count unordered collinear triples and let I(p) be the number containing p. Deleting p yields exactly S(A)-I(p), since deletion creates no triples. In the pinned source's assumed score-11 state, sum I(p)=33 and at least one I(p) is positive. Choosing a maximum-incidence point therefore gives score at most 10. Lexicographic tie-breaking makes initialization deterministic. The bound does not determine the selected point or exact resulting score; those require the proposed future validation. For a zero-score tiny source all incidences are zero and deletion still preserves validity.

For row relocation, the old row u differs from the unique deficient row r and hence has degree two. Moving (u,v) to an absent (r,v) makes row r degree two and row u degree one while preserving every column degree. Its inverse moves the newly added cell back into the now-deficient row u; the old location is absent, so the inverse is legal. Column relocation is the transpose. These moves preserve the degree pattern, not the degree of each individual row or column that changes deficiency. A legal uncolored rectangle replaces two cells in distinct rows and columns with absent crossed cells, preserving every row and column degree; its inverse is the crossed pair. Distinctness, bounds and cardinality follow from the move conditions. A 157-point state need not be represented by two complete permutations; the uncolored helper does not require that representation.

For relocation with remaining set B, the exact delta is -I(old; B)+I(new; B). The helper groups other points by primitive unoriented direction and sums choose(k,2), so it counts each triple through the distinguished point once, including triples on lines containing four or more points. A sequential rectangle deletion/addition telescopes the same score identity: a triple involving both removed points is subtracted at the first deletion only, and a triple involving both new points is added at the last insertion only. Membership and noncoincidence preconditions are required because the helpers do not check them. Determinant recomputation is an appropriate independent tiny oracle.

Every legal row or column transition has proposal probability (1/4)/(2n-1) in either direction; every legal rectangle inverse pair has probability (1/2)/choose(2n-1,2). Illegal choices retained as rejected attempts preserve this symmetry. Thus mathematical Metropolis acceptance min(1, exp(-delta/2)) is consistent without a proposal-ratio correction and permits uphill moves. There is no retention restriction. This establishes local move support and reversibility, not connectivity of the entire state graph, practical mixing, eventual success or exhaustive coverage. Floating-point exp and finite RNG precision are implementation details; do not promote ideal positive probabilities to a numerical reachability proof. The design makes no such global claim.

Validation and persistence are correctly separated from the incremental score. Both full validators must agree on counts, coordinate-canonicalized conflict triples, normalized identity and absence of parse errors; an explicitly checked degree pattern is additionally required because no-three-in-line validation alone does not enforce it. Positive-score saved states remain invalid incumbents. A score-zero witness requires its standalone certificate and both verification reports. Immediate saving after both validators already establish validity correctly survives the soft deadline; beginning a second validator after the soft deadline is not required. Atomic publication and a separate failure receipt prevent disagreement or a partial write from becoming a claimed result.

The single 25-second soft / 30-second owned / 256-MiB run and finite proposal cap bound an exploratory attempt. A stopped run supplies no exclusion, optimum, novelty or global upper bound. A verified 157-point certificate would improve the verified local 156-point baseline. Implementation review should ensure the fixed source hash, proposal cap, RNG ordering, exact helper assumptions, initial/improved invalid-state saving, deadline checks and guard receipt are all concretely pinned and tested before Root allocates an actual run. No blocking mathematical defect found.
