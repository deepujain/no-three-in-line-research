# Checkpoint continuation chain

`research/diagonal_secant_chain.py` is a separate sequential wrapper around the approved checkpoint guard. Its opt-in dispatcher flag is `--diagonal-secant-chain`. The supervisor-supplied `--seconds` sets the aggregate chain ceiling (at most 900 seconds); `--invocation-seconds` independently sets each guard budget (at most 30 seconds), and the sampled RSS ceiling remains at most 256 MiB. The launch window ends at the smaller of 840 seconds and the aggregate ceiling minus 60 seconds; each launch reserves 32 seconds, and at most 26 invocations may be launched. The process start clock includes dispatcher startup. Filesystem operations, corrective receipt writes, lock release and process exit remain a cooperative tail, so these are not hard guarantees against OS stalls or SIGKILL.

The first frontier digest must be externally trusted. A distinct chain lock and checkpoint-specific sticky owner marker prevent cooperating runners from automatically restarting after an interrupted or rejected chain, including when a new ledger directory is supplied. The checkpoint guard still owns its own lock. Each invocation publishes intent before launch, checks guard status, cleanup, finite resource/time values, full source identity, predecessor and result hashes, checkpoint identity, per-slice counters and witness files, then records acceptance in an atomic ledger. It stops on the first invocation containing a witness, timeout, exhaustion, failure or budget limit. A verified witness is retained and checked with both existing geometry verifiers and reflection/count checks. A post-publication failure clears the accepted digest for that invocation and records the published digest separately as unaccepted. An uncooperative writer can bypass advisory locks; a hard kill can orphan the worker's separate session, so a nonclosed marker requires a separate fresh process/ownership audit and explicit new allocation. The runner provides no automatic recovery of that marker. Hashes do not authenticate an untrusted replacement when an attacker also supplies a replacement expected hash.

The combined focused suite passed 63 tests, including n=3/n=5 checkpoint prefixes, witness-stop boundary, sequential invocation limits, aggregate CLI argument routing, malformed counters/resources, mismatched identities/hashes, source/cancellation/deadline failures, interrupted ledger publication and sticky marker behavior. Tests use real tiny DFS subprocesses with the resource helper mocked; they do not prove actual host monitoring.
