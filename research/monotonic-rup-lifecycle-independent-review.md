Input hashes are verified before parsing and again over the complete parsed streams. The parser bounds bytes, records, tokens, line length, variables, retained inference counts and charged work. It keeps original and previously checked clauses for inference across deletions; deletions validate a separate active clause multiset. A clause is normalized to positive/negative literal sets for that bookkeeping, so ordering and repeated literals do not distinguish occurrences; raw literal occurrences still count against storage/work limits. It requires a checked empty addition and complete proof parsing, including records after the empty clause. Rejection, timeout, missing empty clause or identity drift cannot close the family. Retained-clause, literal, time and work caps can reject or time out even a valid proof; they are not proof of satisfiability.

The runner selects one fixed pair by embedded hashes, pins source and runtime identity, launches no SAT solver and at most one checker child, recalculates its five-second slice against the shared deadline after preparation, and requires clean verified child cleanup and a complete, hash-bound acceptance receipt before closure. It preserves rejected receipts. The inherited supervisor and outer guard use sampled ancestry and RSS; neither establishes the absence of an unobserved detached descendant nor excludes a transient memory overshoot. The exact trusted inputs and process boundary remain necessary.
