## Costs, ties and priorities

The exact decomposition is sound. Emptying two rows leaves retained triples, two sets of unary old-pair/new-point triples, and mixed triples consisting of one new point in each row and one retained point. No triple using three new points is possible because two share a row while the third does not. Cross-row equal columns must remain allowed, especially the cross-matrix diagonal. An entry counts every retained point on the line, not merely presence of a line. Signed divisibility of the stated row-intersection formula is exact even for negative denominators.

A current complete pair index contains every retained pair line. Masking both rows and counting remaining bits computes retained score and both unary vectors without building another retained index. For each outer X, adjusted partner costs contain every mixed contribution. The two smallest distinct `(cost,column)` entries in a single scan give minimum cost and the lexicographically smallest sorted partner pair. A smallest singleton is analogous. Enumerating X therefore yields the exact `(U,X,Y)` minimum without retaining all pair-of-pair assignments.

Both load orientations are required for three-point blocks. The clarification's merged stream `(x,)` followed by `(x,y)` for y>x, then the next x, is exactly the sorted union of singleton and pair tuples. It allows immediate first-zero return without violating the global tie rule. Scanning the two orientations in separate batches would not have that property. Four-point blocks use ordinary size-two combinations. Current eligibility means a completed optimum can never be worse than the current state, and strict acceptance avoids neutral cycles. A first zero must receive validation/save priority before optional rebuilding, reporting or scan work; incomplete positive minima never commit.

Priority incidence formulas also hold. Every violated line meets a row at most once because horizontal row occupancy is at most two. Its t points contribute C(t-1,2) triples to each member row, and t-2 to each pair of member rows. Thus I counts exact row incidences and M=I_r+I_s-J_rs counts the exact union removed. A zero I or M cannot improve because all insertion terms are nonnegative. Pair-incidence work over violated lines is bounded by the11026 occupied pairs. A completed Stage1 sweep over positive-I rows establishes a single-row local minimum; a partial sweep does not. Full two-row local-minimum language additionally requires completing every positive-M pair, not merely the32-start cap.
