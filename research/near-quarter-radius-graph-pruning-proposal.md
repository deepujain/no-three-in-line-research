# Cheap near-quarter radius-graph pruning

Mathematical proposal only; no implementation or geometry run. Reuse the completed admissibility mask already produced by packed `_scan`. Add two necessary conditions before MRV: every residual graph component has even demand, and every bipartite component has equal demand on its two sides. This needs no extra orbit push, pair probe, line catalogue or matching solver. Expected usefulness is a hypothesis until measured against the same declared search conditions.

Let `n=2m+1`, use coordinates centered at `(m,m)`, and identify reflected row pairs by radius `u=|y-m|`, with vertices `0,1,...,m`. At an active coherent prefix the exceptional diagonal pair is already selected, at a nonzero radius `d`. All selected four-orbits are quarter-turn invariant. Thus rows at `+u` and `-u` have equal load, and the central row has even load. Define residual demands

`b_0 = (2 - row_load(m))/2`, and `b_u = 2 - row_load(m+u)` for `u>0`.

Noncentral demands are 0, 1 or 2; the central demand is 0 or 1. Each four-orbit comes from two distinct radii `u=|x-m|` and `v=|y-m|`: equal radii are excluded by the two forbidden diagonals. If both are positive, it contributes one point to each of rows `±u, ±v`, so one demand unit at each radius vertex. If one is zero, it contributes two central-row points and one point to each of the other row pair, again one quotient unit at each endpoint. Every four-orbit is therefore an edge `{u,v}`. There are two distinct orbit orientations between positive radii and one edge from zero to each positive radius, giving `2*C(m,2)+m=m²` edge IDs. Keep these as distinct parallel edges; do not deduplicate their capacity.

After the selected diagonal pair and `k` four-orbits, `sum(b)=2(m-k)`. Any full completion selects exactly `m-k` further edges and gives degree exactly `b_u` at every vertex. Every such future orbit is individually admissible against the current prefix, because occupancy, axis and no-three-collinear constraints are monotone under point addition. It therefore appears in the complete existing admissibility mask. The sought completion is a degree-constrained subgraph of this residual multigraph; this is only a necessary relaxation, since conflicts between future orbits are ignored.

Build adjacency for positive-demand vertices from the complete mask. Keep isolated positive-demand vertices. The existing row checks already reject deficient local capacity. In any connected component `C`, all completion edges stay inside `C`, so `sum(b_u for u in C)=2*selected_edges_in_C` must be even. In a bipartite component with sides `L,R`, every selected edge contributes one unit to each side, so `sum(b_L)=sum(b_R)`. An odd cycle merely disables the bipartite equality test for that component; it is not itself a rejection. Parallel edges count twice for degree capacity but do not change connectivity or bipartiteness.

These conditions can reject abstract domains that pass all individual row checks and the total remaining-orbit count. Two disjoint triangles with demand one at each of six vertices have six available edges and need three, with degree two at every vertex, but each component has odd total demand. A `K(2,3)` component with demands `(1,1)` on the two-vertex side and `(1,1,2)` on the other has total demand six, sufficient degree at each vertex and six available edges, but its side totals differ. These are strictness examples for the relaxation, not claims that a tested geometric prefix realizes either graph.

Implementation should be a short postprocessing block in the packed scanner: derive bounded `b`, accumulate adjacency from marked orbit IDs, visit at most `m+1` vertices, and compare component sums and bipartite side sums. At n79 this is at most 40 vertices and 1521 edge IDs; adjacency bitsets may discard duplicate neighbor bits for connectivity only. Poll the common budget while building and traversing; an interruption must return no prune verdict or MRV result. Rejection happens only after the complete geometry scan and before the current node/cursor commits. Passing nodes retain identical MRV row and batch order. A completion cannot be lost, and only dead subtrees disappear.

Do not require a connected completion or a Hamiltonian path: the degree pattern may allow separate cycles, including two-edge cycles from parallel orientations. Do not use a partially scanned mask, count the central row's two-point contribution as two quotient units, infer infeasibility from an odd graph cycle, or alter a saved source identity. The source/policy change must be reviewed as a new derivation; old packed frontiers remain preserved.

The smallest useful validation package is an abstract multigraph oracle with demands in 0..2 and explicit parallel edges; saved tiny valid-prefix checks that compare every new rejection with the already available independent completion oracle; interruption/no-mutation checks; and the existing tiny core suite. Then one Root-allocated equal-node or equal-wall comparison on the same declared slice can measure saved scans versus graph overhead. No separate service, chain protocol or large preflight framework is needed. If the new conditions never reject additional tiny or recorded prefixes, that fact must be retained rather than called an improvement.
