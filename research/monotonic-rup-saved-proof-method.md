## Criterion and canonical deletion semantics

`research/monotonic_rup_checker.py` is independent of the frozen checker, solver and encoder. It uses positive/negative integer masks, with bit0 representing variable1, and repeated bounded-width mask propagation. Tautological database clauses are skipped explicitly; a tautological candidate has contradictory negated assumptions. The candidate is never inserted before its RUP check succeeds. An unchanged assignment pass is a propagation fixed point. Every changing pass assigns another previously unassigned variable.

Every original clause and every checked addition remains permanently in the inference database. Deletions update separate bookkeeping only. Here **exact active multiset** means a multiset of canonical clauses: literal order and duplicate literals inside a clause are normalized away into the positive/negative sets; duplicate clause occurrences retain their multiplicity. A deletion removes exactly one active occurrence of that normalized clause, and a nonexistent or excess deletion is rejected. Thus `1 1 0` and `1 0` denote the same canonical clause for deletion, while two occurrences of either clause permit two deletions. Raw parsed literal multiplicity is still charged conservatively for retained storage and work. This normalization matches the original checker's clause-set convention; it does not claim byte-for-byte deletion matching.

Soundness follows by induction on additions. Initially all inference clauses belong to the original CNF. A RUP addition is entailed by the retained inference database, whose clauses are all original-formula-entailed. Retaining that checked addition preserves the invariant. A checked empty addition therefore proves the original CNF UNSAT even when deletion bookkeeping removes its original reason clauses from the separate active multiset. This accepts a different certificate criterion from strict deletion replay; an acceptance never rewrites the earlier failed receipt into success.
