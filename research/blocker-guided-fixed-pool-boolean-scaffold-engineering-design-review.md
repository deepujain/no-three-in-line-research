## Logical model and removal of traversal duplicates

The 246 selected-arc variables describe an unordered subset directly. Requiring exactly eight and forbidding every pair whose endpoint-row sets or endpoint-column sets intersect gives sixteen distinct old supports forming a matching. This correctly forbids two arcs that reuse the same old point as well as arcs that merely use different old points on the same axis. An OR of deletion variables plus a matching condition alone would not forbid shared supports; the proposed x-level incompatibilities do.

Both directions of q=OR(its support selections) and d=OR(its incident support selections) are present. In particular d cannot introduce free old deletions to satisfy geometry. Two selected arcs cannot name the same planted point because its row would occur in both endpoint-row sets. Therefore exactly eight q and sixteen d are induced. Old matching supports make a planted point's row/column support attribution unique within F. Different Q on the same F remain different solutions.

The four geometric clause groups cover every triple in P=(A minus F) union Q: old-only, one-new/two-old, two-new/one-old, and all-new. The fixed A has exactly five conflicts, so the old-clique and old-points-on-new-pair bounds correctly permit buckets of size four and exclude size five. All required pairs within a larger bucket are emitted. The all-new i<j<k convention emits a collinear triple once. Any valid fixed-pool scaffold satisfies these clauses, and any satisfying semantic assignment yields the intended valid 149 points, 141 retained source points, eight outside-source additions and D17. No residual-geometry claim follows.

This is a material algorithm change: it removes support insertion order from the model. It is not the previously rejected global increasing-arc restriction, an offset replay, or a renamed DFS cap. Equivalent threshold variables and the two semantic OR identities have unique extensions, so they do not reintroduce alternative full assignments for one x selection. No model enumeration is proposed.

## Cardinality and arithmetic

The four clauses for z iff a OR (x AND b) give both implications: a or (x and b) forces z, while z forces both (a or x) and (a or b), whose conjunction is a or (x and b). With the given boundary constants, induction fixes each z(i,j) to the appropriate threshold. Asserting threshold eight and denying threshold nine enforces exactly eight selections. Constant simplification must preserve this equivalence and uniqueness in the eventual emitter.

Independently checked counts: 467 semantic variables plus sum(min(i,9), i=1..246)=2178 auxiliaries gives 2645 total variables. At most 8714 counter clauses and 23960 counter literals are correct. C(246,2)=30135 bounds incompatibility clauses; q equivalences contribute at most 310 clauses/802 literals, and d equivalences 649/1633. For L=2,3,4, bucket-cost partitions give maximum blocker-pair counts 3,6,7. The audited histogram thus gives 416 one-new clauses. At most four old points per new-pair line gives 8064 two-new clauses; C(64,3)=41664 bounds all-new clauses. Summing the declared groups gives 89957 clauses and 237112 literal occurrences.

For variable IDs at most 2645, six bytes per signed-literal token including its following space is sufficient. Two terminating bytes per clause and a 17-byte longest header give at most 1602603 DIMACS bytes. The temporary body plus final file is below 3.21 decimal MB. These are representation bounds, not actual generated counts or a solver-memory prediction. Per-clause streaming and a bounded copy buffer avoid retaining Python objects for the full formula. The actual emitter must fail closed on any variable/clause/literal/byte-bound violation and record exact counts and the immutable CNF hash.

## Reuse, scope and resource boundary

The proposal correctly distinguishes saved data from hashes: the old report stores the audited 64 pool entries, not live bucket/support/table bytes or a DFS frontier. It uses those pinned entries directly, recomputes only 64 partitions/246 supports/2016 pair tables, checks saved L and canonical support/table hashes, and directly verifies the saved pool matching/cover. The accepted audit supplies the original ranking evidence. This requires at most 10048 direction visits plus the declared table work, without scanning all 6083 outside cells, reranking, changing the pool or replaying any of the 4096 DFS examinations. A mismatch is an error, not permission to derive a replacement pool.

One immutable CNF, one fresh SAT invocation and at most one returned scaffold/root check keep the proposed experiment distinct and finite. A valid 149 diagnostic must be saved immediately after its two agreeing validators. A fresh deadline check precedes optional root work; a second validator crossing the soft deadline leaves the diagnostic saved and starts no root. A failed first root does not justify a second model, blocking clause, solve call, retry or completion DFS. A root-ready artifact still carries the untested-joint-residual-geometry limitation.

The revised native contract addresses the initial engineering gaps: SAT has at most min(10 seconds, remaining soft time minus 3); a subsequent independent checker has at most min(5 seconds, remaining soft time minus 1). Processes run sequentially, retain the already owned worker's PGID/SID and must be fully reaped with no tracked descendants before any next native launch. TERM grace is at most 0.1 seconds, then KILL/reap/cleanup at most another 0.4 seconds; uncertain cleanup ends native work and leaves final owned-group cleanup to the unchanged outer guard. The implementation must test this per-native-child lifecycle rather than assume the existing root-worker tracker can be reused unchanged for a child that inherits the worker's group.

The fixed limits are 8 MiB for the proof file, 64 KiB each for stdout and stderr per native process, and 32 KiB of model text. A complete 2645-variable model fits within that text allowance, but the parser must still reject duplicates, omissions, out-of-range variables and contradictions rather than fill missing assignments implicitly. Streams require bounded reads and active cap handling; truncation cannot become a model or proof. The proof-file bound must be hard and demonstrated, not merely a sampled size observation. Native watches, learned clauses and checker allocations have no static RSS guarantee from these file/CNF bounds, so the owned 30-second/sampled-256-MiB guard remains necessary.

Raw UNSAT, interrupted native work, output/proof cap, unchecked/truncated proof, failed proof checking or expired checker budget gives no closure. Only an independent checked proof bound to the exact emitted CNF may close the fixed-pool scaffold family. It closes neither other pools nor all D17 cycles nor the 157-point problem. Proof checker identity, proof format and hard-cap support are genuine pre-allocation prerequisites; if no available pinned backend/checker meets them, stop or revise the design rather than install a fallback or waive proof requirements.
