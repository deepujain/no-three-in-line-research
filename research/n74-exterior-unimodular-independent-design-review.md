For a valid 148-point source, at most two points share each level of any nonzero integer linear form, so at least 74 distinct integer levels and width at least 73 are required. Homogeneity and subadditivity then prove the mod-3 injection: if distinct admissible u and v shared a residue, `u-v=3w` would have width at least 219, exceeding the sum of their at-most-74 widths. Including zero yields at most eight signed directions, or four opposite pairs. The ordinary horizontal and vertical directions are present; at most six unordered pairs and hence five nonordinary pairs remain. These deductions depend on the source's full validity, not merely axis capacity.

Canonicalizing each row vector's sign and row order is complete up to target-board reflection and transposition: transform the translation and added cell along with the frame, leaving their integer preimage q unchanged. For width 73, 74 occupied levels must each carry two points; there are two placements and one deficient level per placement. For width 74, the unique placement has total spare capacity two, giving at most two deficient levels. Thus each coordinate has at most two placement/deficiency choices and their product yields at most four candidates across all translations of a frame, not four for each translation. Five frames give at most 20 candidate preimages before occupancy, exterior and duplicate filters. Distinct candidates can be checked against at most C(148,2) old pairs each; unimodular affine maps preserve old collinearity and integer invertibility.

Requiring q outside U excludes all preimages that could lie in any such fixed-board scan, independently of which offset was used. The public scan's source-record identity remains unknown, and this conservative exclusion does not assert that any interior q was actually checked for record 1. A non-axis frame alone does not imply an exterior q or disjointness from other prior work.
