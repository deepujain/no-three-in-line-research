# Independent near-quarter radius-graph proposal review

Reviewed proposal SHA-256 `db7bdf4c3ab4e8082546d437df557dcca2634bc18e3539bf0b61864e3a613261`. Its component parity and bipartite balance conditions are sound necessary conditions for a coherent active near-quarter prefix and a complete individually admissible orbit mask. No implementation or numerical execution was performed in this review.

The noncentral row pair has equal occupancy. A noncentral/noncentral four-orbit adds one point to each signed row at both radii, consuming one demand at each vertex. A central/noncentral orbit adds two points to the central row and one in each signed noncentral row, consuming one unit of the halved central demand and one noncentral unit. The selected diagonal pair consumes one unit at its nonzero radius; hence total remaining quotient demand is twice the number of remaining four-orbits. Distinct positive radii have two different orbit orientations, so they must remain distinct parallel capacity edges; merging their adjacency bits for connectivity alone is safe. Equal radii are excluded by this declared off-diagonal family.

Any valid completion uses a subset of the complete individually admissible mask, which cannot cross graph components. Every selected edge contributes two units of component demand; in a bipartite component it contributes one unit to each side. Thus odd component demand or unequal bipartite side sums preclude completion. Odd cycles, disconnected solutions and parallel two-edge cycles are not themselves failures. Future orbit conflicts make these necessary conditions, not sufficiency tests. The abstract examples demonstrate strictness only for multigraph relaxations and do not establish reachable geometric prefixes or runtime benefit.

Before integration, validate the mapping against actual orbit IDs, retain isolated positive-demand vertices, preserve parallel multiplicity for degree capacity, and reject no partial scan when interrupted. The proposed independent tiny completion oracle, abstract multigraph enumeration, interruption/state tests and controlled same-slice timing are appropriate. A policy or source change needs a new identity and preserved old frontiers. Approve this mathematical proposal for a separately reviewed implementation, not as evidence of speed or authorization to run larger geometry.
