## Cubic objective and legal support

The objective is correctly cubic even when four rows may change. Every collinear conflict is a three-point set, classified by zero, one, two or three new points. These cases yield retained base, four unary sums, six pair-cross sums and four ternary sums indexed by the3-subsets of the four selected rows. There is no four-new term. Same-row point pairs lie on a horizontal line and cannot form a conflict with an off-row point; row capacities exclude three points on one row. Equal columns across different selected rows remain legal and make the vertical ternary entries indispensable. Larger-line multiplicity is already counted by summing all point triples; a separate four-point penalty would double-count or change the task.

Four rows have total8 when all are full, or total7 when they contain the unique singleton. Four columns per row give6⁴=1,296 total-eight assignments and4×4×6³=3,456 total-seven assignments. Including each row's old columns makes the current assignment feasible. Singleton orientation may move only within the selected block while preserving one singleton globally. A selected block can improve with fewer than four changed rows; selection alone proves no four-row necessity.

The proposed eligibility condition is finite and meaningful as a heuristic: two distinct K-positive triples sharing exactly two row labels have a four-row union. With at most64 positive keys there are at most C(64,2)=2,016 unordered pairs before rejecting wrong overlaps and deduplicating unions. This is an upper bound, not evidence that any such block exists on the actual64 state or yields an improvement. The completed triple's removal-dependent unary domains generally differ from independently chosen four-row domains.

## Separation criterion

For one fixed state and common current-inclusive domains on four rows, the four faces holding one row at its old columns cover every assignment changing at most three rows. If a strict four-row optimum is below current U while each face minimum equals U, every strict improvement in that common-domain neighborhood must change all four rows. The argument also covers one- and two-row changes because they lie on multiple faces. Current inclusion supplies the ≤U direction; exhaustive exact face minima supply the ≥U direction. All compatible singleton orientations are needed when the fixed row is full; holding the old singleton fixed leaves three full rows.

This proves a common-domain support barrier only. It proves neither a full-grid three-row local minimum nor a barrier under independently recalculated production domains. The additional production comparator is therefore necessary for the memo's stronger promotion rule: every eligible K-positive production triple, with its own original selector/domain rules, must also fail to improve. Conversely, production failure does not replace the face test; a common-domain face might expose a column omitted by that production triple's unary selection. Checking both prevents domain changes or selector changes from being misreported as four-row coordination.

The memo correctly requires both strict score reduction and exact direct-score/table/current-inclusion/lexical-tie checks. A four-row result changing fewer than four rows cannot pass a genuine face separation check. A positive control stub or a root-reported two-row change in a selected triple supplies no evidence for this mechanism.

## Abstract check and small-grid evidence

The stated binary cycle energy is nonnegative and uses only unary and pair terms. At all-zero it is1; at all-one it is0. Every proper nonempty flipped subset has at least two cycle boundaries. If it includes vertex0 the unary contribution is0 and total energy is at least2; otherwise unary is1 and total is at least3. Thus all four bits must change to lower energy from all-zero. This algebra proves the abstract example without enumerating its sixteen vectors. It establishes no embedding into determinant collinearity or a legal row-valid grid.

A bounded sixteen-vector abstract check would be harmless future unit-test work if explicitly requested, but adds no missing geometric evidence. Existing n4 examples can serve as negative controls only.
