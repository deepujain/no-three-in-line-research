# Independent design review: blocker-guided eight-arc scaffold

Decision: PASS as a bounded scaffold-feasibility design, subject to separate implementation, independent source/tiny review and lead allocation. Reviewed `research/blocker-guided-eight-arc-scaffold157-design.md` SHA-256 `cdc1d5ac697f0698ff6df63a68852599c7fb952cf82f18b8d367cfc69a024d6b`. No fixed n79 point validation, scoring, blocker enumeration, matching or candidate generation occurred in this review.

For an outside point q, primitive unoriented direction groups partition A. It is safe against A minus F exactly when at most one member of each direction bucket remains. Thus every old blocking pair must be hit, and the unrestricted minimum deletions are the sum of |B|-1 over nontrivial buckets. Since A has exactly five collinear triples, a five-point bucket would itself contribute ten triples, so no bucket exceeds four. For k<=4, C(k,2)<=2(k-1), establishing the stated 32 pair-blocker bound for an L<=16 pool point. This L filter is necessary for a 16-deletion completion, but choosing only the first 64 points by L/coordinate is a deliberately incomplete restriction.

Each q=(row(a),col(b)) is outside S and therefore outside A. If its distinct old supports shared a row or a column, one of the supports would equal q; hence a and b are compatible. Requiring all sixteen supports to be a matching makes every planted q's row and column distinct from those of the other planted cells and keeps the scaffold within the original axis capacities. F is exactly those supports. R=A minus F contains 141 cells; F hits all old conflicts, the per-q pair blockers eliminate triples with one new point, forcing old points on each new pair's line eliminates triples with two new points, and the new-triple test eliminates triples with three new points. These cases cover every triple. The scaffold has 149 points and exactly 17 effective deletions from S because A=S minus one, F has 16 source cells and all eight q are outside S. It is a valid partial construction if verified, not a 157-point result.

A sixteen-cycle consists of alternating a_i->b_i planted arcs and b_i->a_tau(i) residual arcs when any eight alternating arcs around the cycle are chosen. The full permutation's cycles have twice the component lengths of tau; one 16-cycle is equivalent to one 8-cycle. Thus the abstract eight-disjoint-arc representation can represent any full 16-cycle, while pool truncation, first-four-root limits and attempt cap remove that completeness. At a full assignment all sixteen freed row and column capacities are restored, preserving both original deficiencies.

For a partial state, every final F must include T=F union U, so rejecting |T|>16 or nonmatching T is sound. For each selected q, its direction buckets are disjoint, and the sum of max(0,|B minus T|-1) is a valid lower bound on further deletions for that q. Taking the maximum of those per-q lower bounds is safe; summing them across q could double-count shared deletions and is not justified. The selected unmet forced point, pair blocker, or old conflict triple must eventually be supplied by at least one future support arc. Branching on a satisfying arc without a global increasing-index rule can represent any ordering needed to meet that requirement; repeated orderings are possible and consume the shared cap. This argument is conditional on a complete branch over the retained pool; neither the top-64 selection nor cap failures exclude all D17 cycles.

A pool matching below eight rules out an eight-cell Q within that selected pool, provided the graph is complete and its matching/cover certificate verifies. At a root, a verified residual perfect matching is necessary but not sufficient for a single cycle; an explicit 8-cycle is sufficient for that root graph's simultaneous row/column/cycle condition. Its remaining eight cells are each safe with the existing 149 points, but their pairwise and triple interactions are not tested by that certificate. A passing root therefore establishes a root-ready scaffold and plan, not a full valid endpoint or a guarantee a completion exists.

The operation arithmetic is internally consistent: 6083*157=955031 direction visits, C(64,2)*(157+64)=445536 pair-table checks, 4*C(149,2)*79=3484216 root line visits, and 4*7!*8=161280 worst-case cycle edge checks. These are upper bounds only if loops implement the stated unique lattice-cell visitation, scan caps and one shared 4096 arc-attempt counter; they are not measured runtime or memory. A root interrupted during mask, domain, matching, cycle checking, or either validator is incomplete. Persist a fully dual-validated ready scaffold immediately if validator two finishes after the soft deadline; report a later guard stop separately. No automatic completion DFS, broadened pool, alternate ordering or retry is authorized by the design.

Before any actual gate, tiny exhaustive tests must verify bucket and four-triple-class semantics, source/axis invariants, residual cycle expansion, same-F/different-Q handling, overlap-safe lower bound and requirement-driven ordering against a brute-force oracle. Test deadline/cap/rollback and dual-validation/write boundaries separately, and retain exact source and input hashes. Any eventual negative result is restricted to the visited prefixes of the first selected 64 cells and at most four distinct root scaffolds; the existence of a D17 construction remains open.
