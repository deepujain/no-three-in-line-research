# n71 near-quarter fixture to n79: finite extension question

The saved n71 fixture is a valid 142-point full near-quarter set, not a construction theorem for n79. Its existence alone supplies no general lift. A uniform integer scale of two or more cannot embed its full coordinate span 0..70 into 0..78; scale one plus translation preserves collinearity, and the centered translation `(x,y) -> (x+4,y+4)` is the natural symmetry-preserving choice. It carries the board center (35,35) to (39,39), keeps the existing 35 four-orbits and one main-diagonal half-turn pair, and retains exact validity. It contains only 142 points, so it is not yet a 158-point configuration.

Because a full valid n71 set has exactly two points in every old row and column, the translated points saturate rows and columns 4..74. Any zero-deletion 158-point extension must place all 16 new points in the boundary subgrid with both coordinates in `{0,1,2,3,75,76,77,78}`. Eight boundary rows each require two new points; the same holds for columns. The near-quarter family requires four additional complete four-orbits and forbids both diagonals for those orbits. The inherited main-diagonal pair also blocks adding any other main-diagonal point by collinearity. Thus naive centered embedding or scaling alone cannot fill the target, and arbitrary border insertion is not justified.

There is nevertheless a very small **unverified finite extension question**, not a general lift. Around center 39, the boundary has four absolute coordinate radii 36,37,38,39. Allowed four-orbits correspond to two distinct radii, with two orientations per unordered pair: 12 possible orbit IDs. Each orbit uses one point in each signed row of its two radii; quarter-turn symmetry enforces the same column loads. Selecting four orbits to give degree two at each of four radii has exactly 51 raw possibilities: three simple four-cycles with two orientation choices for each edge (3×2⁴=48), or three pairings with both parallel orientations selected on each paired edge. This count imposes row/column occupancy and the orbit family only; it does **not** check collinearity within or between new orbits or with the 142 old points. Old secants can forbid candidate additions, and a valid n71 fixture gives no reason to assume any of the 51 passes them.

A later separately authorized, independently reviewed direct check of these 51 boundary sets against both exact validators would settle only zero-deletion extensions of this specific centered fixture. It would be a concrete low-cost lead if the pilot chooses to spend that work; this memo did not construct those sets or perform host geometry. If all fail, stop this naive lift rather than inferring that n79 has no near-quarter solution: moving or deleting original points, nonuniform embeddings and other n71 witnesses are outside its scope. A passing 158-point set would need complete independent verification and fresh literature review before any novelty claim.
