# n=74 to n=75 one-point extension: focused prior scope

Read-only check 27 November 2026; no numerical scan, source decoding or fixture validation. The [Flammenkamp history](https://wwwhomes.uni-bielefeld.de/achim/no3in/readme.html) attributes an n=74 full rot4 configuration to Thomas Prellberg on 20 July 2026. The primary archive [n74_rot4.few](https://wwwhomes.uni-bielefeld.de/~achim/no3in/download/configurations/n74_rot4.few) displays two encoded records; exact record selection, byte hash and point-set identity must be established before source use. The archive itself should be fetched as bytes and a selected record decoded and checked with two exact validators, rather than assuming which line the following report used.

A public [EinsteinArena report](https://einsteinarena.com/problems/no-three-in-line-75/threads/271) describes decoding a Prellberg 148-point n=74 configuration, embedding it in n=75, and testing every free cell for a one-point extension. It reports none, including the eligible outside corner of that embedding. The post does not identify its encoded record, full point-set hash or exact embedding offset. Thus the report indicates substantial overlap with the proposed four-corner family, but its identity and correspondence to either archive record and all four embeddings are not established by the available text. Do not claim a distinct experiment or an independently verified negative result on this evidence. If record identity is established, rotational symmetry may relate translated corners, but the required symmetry and coordinate equivalence must be checked rather than assumed.

The prior n=75 survey identified 148 as an attributable available baseline but no attributable 149 witness; this is not a comprehensive priority search or impossibility proof. A different selected archive record would still require a source-specific prior-work assessment. No numerical allocation is recommended merely to repeat a reported tested family.
