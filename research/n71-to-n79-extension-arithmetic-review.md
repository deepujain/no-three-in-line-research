# Independent arithmetic review of the centered fixed-fixture extension

The 51-candidate count and finite completeness argument are sound for zero-deletion near-quarter-turn extensions of the specified centered n71 fixture. This is proof review only: no candidate point sets, secants, validators, n79 geometry or search were executed.

Translation by `(4,4)` maps old center `(35,35)` to `(39,39)` and preserves all collinearity relations and quarter-turn orbits. The known 142-point fixture has exactly two points in each old row and column, so the translated configuration saturates every row and column from 4 through 74. A valid extension cannot add a point in any of those rows or columns. Thus both coordinates of each of the required sixteen new points must lie in `{0,1,2,3,75,76,77,78}`.

The inherited 35 four-orbits and diagonal pair remain present. Within the declared near-quarter family, the existing pair fixes the unique exceptional pair, so the sixteen added points must be four complete off-diagonal four-orbits. The allowed boundary coordinates have absolute radii 36, 37, 38, and 39 about center 39. Equal coordinate radii lie on one of the two excluded diagonals. For each unordered pair of distinct radii there are exactly two quarter-turn orbit orientations, giving twelve candidate orbits in total.

An orbit associated with distinct radii u and v contributes one point to each signed row at u and v, and the corresponding column loads are identical by quarter-turn symmetry. Requiring two points in every outer row is therefore exactly degree two at every vertex of the four-vertex multigraph, with each candidate orbit a distinct edge and at most two parallel edges per pair. Four selected orbits give four edges.

Every loopless degree-two multigraph decomposes into cycles of length at least two. On four vertices, only a four-cycle or two disjoint two-cycles can use all vertices. There are three undirected simple four-cycles, each with two independent orientation choices per edge, giving `3*2^4=48` selections. There are three perfect matchings; each produces two two-cycles by selecting both available parallel edges on each matched pair, giving three more. The cases are disjoint and exhaustive, hence exactly 51 candidate sets.

These are only family and occupancy conditions. Collinearity among added points or with old points still needs both exact geometry validators. A failed exhaustive scan would exclude only zero-deletion near-quarter extensions of this particular centered fixture. It would not exclude unrestricted zero-deletion extensions, other fixtures/embeddings, modifications of old points, or n79 solutions in general.
