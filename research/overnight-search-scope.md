# Overnight near-quarter search: scope and next decisions

The packed n79 search targets **158 points** in a restricted near-quarter-turn family. At n=79, m=39: the declared model contains 1,521 quarter-turn four-point orbit choices away from both diagonals and 39 half-turn pairs on the main diagonal. A full candidate selects 39 four-orbits and exactly one diagonal pair, giving 39×4+2=158 points; the anti-diagonal and center are absent. The exceptional pair means the whole set is half-turn invariant but generally not quarter-turn invariant. Any accepted 158-point witness must pass both independent exact geometry verifiers and the family checks. Since each of the 79 rows holds at most two points, such a witness proves the unrestricted finite maximum D(79)=158. A novelty or priority claim additionally requires current independent literature checking.

This engine does **not directly enumerate 157-point candidates**: all its represented sets have even cardinality. A valid 158-point witness yields valid 157-point subsets by deletion, but the reviewed center-absent half-turn lemma rules out using one deletion to repair an invalid represented 158-point set into a valid 157-point set. It does not rule out unrestricted 157-point sets or other symmetry classes. The previously verified embedded seed supplies 156 points at n79; a separately verified 157-point candidate improves that local baseline but does not prove optimality or automatically establish novelty.

The last independently audited primary HEAD before the current run had 511 row-batch logical nodes, no witness and zero completed diagonal slices; it was still on ID 1522, the edge pair. The proposed middle probe fixes ID 1541, the pair (19,19),(59,59), while retaining the complete four-orbit and row ordering. It is one of 39 declared diagonal-pair slices, not necessarily 1/39 of search time, feasible solutions, or tree nodes. The two IDs partition candidates by their unique chosen diagonal pair, but raw possible 39-orbit selections per slice, `C(1521,39)`, vastly overcount legal sets and are not a denominator for traversed DFS nodes. Neither 511 nodes nor a 250-node middle probe yields a meaningful fraction of the finite tree, and unequal slice costs are possible. The current primary run may have advanced since that audit; use its terminal receipts rather than this historical counter for current status.

This family is a structural hypothesis: its symmetry can exclude every actual solution even if D(79)=158. Its exact feasibility and practical runtime are unknown. The reported approximately 19× readonly speedup at a matched 11-node prefix measures that specific implementation comparison, not likelihood of a witness or a full-tree completion time. A root preflight measures construction and one root only. Timeout, a node quota, failed monitoring, or no witness does not establish infeasibility. Even complete slice exhaustion would be scoped to that declared restricted family and should be independently replayed or supported by a checkable proof before a formal UNSAT claim.
