# Proposed diagonal-reflection secant DFS for 157 points

Prepared 2026-11-27 by Mathematical Reviewer, gpt-6.1-terra/high per parent assignment. This is a mathematical design and correctness plan, not an implemented algorithm, numerical result, or novelty claim. No search or large enumeration was run. It is independent of the half-turn-only 158-point near-quarter-turn branch and remains restricted to a particular reflection family.

## Proved orbit and deficit facts

Let G={0,…,78}² and T(x,y)=(y,x). Suppose S⊂G is T-invariant, has 157 points, and has no three collinear. Every row and column has at most two points. Since 157=2·79−1, exactly one row has load one and all others load two; the same is true of columns. Reflection interchanges row i and column i, so the deficient row and column share an index d.

The off-diagonal T-orbits have two points and each diagonal point is fixed. Odd |S| requires an odd number of fixed diagonal points. At most two selected points can lie on the main diagonal, so exactly one is selected: (t,t). Thus S consists of exactly **78 off-diagonal pairs and one diagonal singleton**. The indices t and d need not coincide. The explicit valid n=3 example `{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,2),(2,1)}` has t=0,d=2; the valid n=4 example `{(0,1),(1,0),(0,2),(2,0),(1,2),(2,1),(3,3)}` has t=d=3. They are included as correctness fixtures, not evidence for n=79.

Each off-diagonal orbit {(i,j),(j,i)} lies on the anti-diagonal i+j=s. Two distinct selected orbits with the same s create four points on that line, and an orbit with s=2t plus (t,t) creates a triple. These restrictions are necessary, but all other directions still require exact geometry.

Equivalently, represent each selected off-diagonal orbit as an edge {i,j} in a simple graph on the 79 coordinate indices. Its degree at i is `2 − [i=d] − [i=t]`. If d=t, vertex d is isolated and the rest are cycles; if d≠t, d and t are the two degree-one endpoints of a path and the remaining components are cycles. This is a necessary degree characterization, not a guarantee of noncollinearity; parallel edges are impossible for this particular representation.

The generic row bound and line-capacity formulation are documented in [Prellberg, arXiv:2602.07751, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07751#S2). The reflection deductions above follow directly from orbit parity and row/column capacities; they are also reviewed locally in `research/symmetry-review.md`. Flammenkamp's [database readme](https://wwwhomes.uni-bielefeld.de/achim/no3in/readme.html) describes historical branch-and-bound search, but the proposed algorithm below is our adaptation, not a claim about that program. Source access date 2026-11-27.

## Proposed exact search

Enumerate all 79 choices of d and all 79 choices of t; do not impose t=d or t≠d. Insert the chosen singleton (t,t), then branch on off-diagonal orbits. Maintain exact target row loads `2−[i=d]` and the identical column targets, selected point set, selected orbit count, and a reversible per-cell secant counter. Each remaining pair orbit must be disjoint from selected cells, fit both axis capacities and avoid creating a collinear triple. Static same-anti-diagonal and `s=2t` exclusions are safe necessary filters. Each successful full state has 78 selected pair orbits and the fixed singleton.

For the secant counter, `blocked[p]` counts unordered pairs of selected points whose full Euclidean line includes p. To try an orbit, add its points one at a time: reject a point if blocked, update all grid cells on every newly formed pair's full maximal line, then check the next point against the updated counter. Check row/column capacity and roll back **all** changes on rejection. Undo uses exact reverse insertion order and decrements precisely the contributions added for the removed point's pairs. Primitive signed directions and line traversal must handle both directions and every slope, including horizontal and vertical. This procedure detects old-old-new, old-new-new and internal orbit triples; its correctness depends on preserving the counter invariant at every step.

At each search node choose an underfilled row and enumerate **all** currently admissible unselected pair orbits that contribute a point to that row. A minimum-remaining-values choice is permitted for ordering. If an extension exists, at least one of its as-yet-unselected pair orbits contributes to that row and is currently admissible: otherwise the extension would violate monotone capacity or geometry. Branching on all choices thus covers every extension. Prune only on proved necessary bounds, such as an underfilled row with no admissible orbit, insufficient possible remaining row/column capacity, an excessive pair count, or too few compatible pair orbits to reach 78. A graph matching/degree relaxation could strengthen this only after a separate proof and tests. Different insertion orders may visit the same set; duplicates are acceptable. Do **not** impose a global increasing-orbit order or other symmetry breaking during dynamic row choice unless completeness is separately proved.

A completed traversal of all d,t slices would concern only T-invariant configurations. One timeout, one failed slice, a search heuristic plateau or a full traversal of a different symmetry family has no unrestricted infeasibility consequence. A valid output must contain 157 points, have both independently checked determinant/slope geometry and the declared reflection, and undergo a fresh literature/priority check. An exhaustively reported negative result requires trustworthy complete traversal, independent replay or a checkable proof before formal presentation.

## Memory accounting and performance hypothesis

At n=79 there are C(79,2)=3,081 off-diagonal orbit candidates and 79 singleton diagonal choices; each d,t slice fixes its singleton, and at most 79 total orbits are selected. A packed `uint16` blocker count array over 6,241 grid cells costs 12,482 bytes. At most C(157,2)=12,246 selected pairs can contribute to one cell, so 16 bits suffice **if** increments/undo are correctly bounded. A packed cell-to-orbit map at two bytes per cell is another 12,482 bytes; point, axis-load and traversal stacks are additional.

If each formed pair is walked over at most 79 grid cells, a full root-to-leaf path incurs at most 79·C(157,2)=967,434 counter increments. Storing one 16-bit index per increment is about 1.85 MiB plus offsets; recomputing exact reverse updates from the selected orbit stack can avoid retaining that full log, at extra time cost. These numbers describe hypothetical **packed arrays only**, not measured process RSS. Python objects, callbacks, allocator overhead and optional pruning data can dominate; an unbounded visited-state cache or learned-cut database would defeat the bounded-live-state objective. A bounded DFS may still visit an infeasible number of nodes or spend substantial time updating secants. No speedup over the existing SAT branch is asserted.

## Required gates before n=79 use

1. On n=3 and n=4, enumerate every cell subset independently and compare the complete valid T-invariant 2n−1 sets with the union of all d,t DFS slices. For n=5, exhaust every reflection-orbit assignment (15 Boolean orbits) and compare full sets and all admissible/pruned partial states against independent determinant checks. Include the explicit t=d and t≠d examples above, same-anti-diagonal collisions, vertical and negative slopes, and internal-new-orbit triples.
2. After every add, rejected tentative add, and undo in exhaustive tiny states, reconstruct all pair-line counters from scratch and compare every cell, point set, degree and occupancy. Assert counter width/nonnegativity and exact restoration. Any discrepancy blocks further work. Independently check any emitted witness with both existing geometry verifiers and the reflection map.
3. In a separately scheduled worker slot, compare deterministic bounded DFS and the existing diagonal-reflection SAT on the same known small odd instances and target, under equal measured limits. Record nodes, dead-end/prune reasons, time, per-node work, peak RSS and full output certificates. A 30-second/256-MiB per-process preflight with aggregate margin is a proposed guardrail, not a guarantee. Move to n=79 only after independent code review, all correctness gates, and a measured acceptable preflight; do not silently drop slopes or slices to meet a resource cap.

This is a path toward a new 157-point witness, not a statement that one exists or is unpublished. No external messages, code changes, or numerical tasks were performed for this proposal.
