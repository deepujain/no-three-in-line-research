## What would count as a material future design

Two source-level facts identify possible research directions, without approving either implementation:

- **Exact state uniqueness.** F, Q, the forced-old mask U, old row/column masks and the next deterministic requirement are functions of the selected support-arc set. Different insertion orders of that same set have identical remaining choices. A correctly designed exact-set DAG traversal could avoid re-exploring equivalent subtrees. It must distinguish exhausted states from interrupted/incomplete ones, bound storage, preserve all scaffold families and prove its ordinal/closure semantics. The current20 admissions do not reveal how many such duplicates occurred, so no measured speedup or additional progress follows from this observation.
- **Requirement-projected branching.** Support incidence indexes and compatibility masks could expose only arcs that can hit the current requirement while respecting mandatory supports and axes. This can remove work on statically impossible records, especially near the final slot. Preserving virtual charges for skipped records would leave the old4096 frontier unchanged; charging only surviving arcs changes the work unit and requires new physical-operation caps. Merely renaming a much larger raw-prefix budget as4096 useful attempts would be a cap increase in disguise. A proper design needs an independently checked completeness argument and bounded costs for producing and updating the candidate projection.

Neither point is a finished selection algorithm, benchmark, measured bottleneck reduction or allocation request. Any future proposal must materially change uniqueness, requirement feasibility or geometric selection, retain exact D17 and scaffold-only labeling unless it explicitly rederives a different family, and pass independent design plus source/tiny review before Root considers actual work. No actual prefix analysis is requested to justify such a proposal during this curtailed task.
