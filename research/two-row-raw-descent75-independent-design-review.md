For two emptied rows, each final triple has zero, one, two or three new points. Zero-new triples contribute U(R); one-new terms are the unary pair-of-retained costs. Two new points in one row cannot be collinear with a retained off-row point, while one point in each row contributes one mixed triple for each collinear retained point, including vertical `C[x,x]`. Three selected points from two rows necessarily include two in one row and a third in the other, so cannot be collinear. Thus the stated decomposition is exact. For each fixed first-row set X, summing its cross terms into each second-row unary cost makes choosing the one or two smallest distinct `(cost,column)` entries exact. Enumerating X and comparing the stated score/lexicographic tuple covers the 2/2 case and both 1/2 orientations, including the current state.

For each violated nonhorizontal line, every row contains at most one of its points. A member point participates in C(t-1,2) triples; two selected member rows share exactly t-2 triples. Inclusion-exclusion gives `M[r,s]=I[r]+I[s]-J[r,s]` as the exact count destroyed by emptying both rows. A zero incidence cannot yield a strict decrease because subsequent additions only add triples. The proposed eligibility and priority ordering therefore do not omit an improving row or pair when the corresponding sweep completes. A started/capped partial sweep is not a local-minimum certificate. A found zero needs immediate dual validation and save; a failed or bounded descent proves no general exclusion.
