# Small residual radius cuts: arithmetic proposal

Status: design-only. No implementation, numerical execution, packed genesis or frontier modification is authorized by this note.

[Prellberg, §3 equation (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07751#S3) gives the weighted row equalities for the near-quarter orbit family. The rule below is our derived necessary condition, not a rule or speed claim attributed to that paper. For the frozen packed DFS, use the complete individually admissible orbit mask and its existing residual radius multigraph. Each selected future orbit is an edge joining distinct radius vertices; the central quotient demand is already halved by the existing graph mapping. Let d(v) be residual degree demand, e(U) the number of *distinct available orbit edges* internal to vertex subset U, and δ(U) the available edges with exactly one endpoint in U. Every possible completion must satisfy

`d(U) <= 2*e(U) + min(d(V\U), |δ(U)|)`.

Indeed a selected internal edge supplies two units to U and a selected crossing edge supplies one, consumes one unit of outside demand, and is drawn from the available crossing edges. Count parallel orbit edges separately: each is a distinct selectable orbit. Other geometric incompatibilities only tighten this optimistic bound. A strict violation permits rejection; passing does not establish feasibility. Check every 2- or 3-vertex subset of positive-demand vertices, not all subsets. At n79 there are at most 40 quotient vertices and therefore 780 pairs plus 9,880 triples, 10,660 total. Compute edge multiplicities and incident counts once from the existing complete mask; an interrupted scan or unverified/incomplete mask yields no verdict. The actual cost and viable hit rate are unknown.

A distinguishing *abstract* graph: vertices a,b,c have demand 2; d,e have demand 1. Available edges are ab, ad, be, cd, ce, de. Every vertex has enough incident edges. The graph is connected with even total demand eight and has triangle c-d-e, so component parity and bipartite-balance checks pass. For U={a,b,c}, d(U)=6, there is one internal edge, and outside demand is two; the right-hand side is 2+2=4, so the new bound rejects. If six distinct edge colors are assigned, the reflection scanner's global/component color counts also pass its four-edge requirement. This is a multigraph-relaxation example, not a claim that the graph arises from an actual geometric prefix.

## Comparison with existing work

`research/near_quarter_rowbatch_core.py::_scan` uses the completed admissibility mask, global orbit count, per-row optimistic load, and `radius_obstruction` for component parity and bipartite balance; `research/near_quarter_radius_graph.py` does not check subset cut capacity. The separately reviewed reflection-family `research/diagonal-rowbatch-residual-graph-pruning-proposal.md` and production scanner add row degree, component parity, bipartite side balance, and global/component anti-diagonal color counts; they explicitly do not implement general subset-capacity tests. Their color rule applies to a different symmetry family and is not asserted for this near-quarter graph. Thus the proposed local cut is logically distinct, though it may rarely reject an actually reachable near-quarter prefix.

The reflection residual implementation was slower on its measured prefix: 5,000 nodes in 10.364 seconds versus 9,000 control nodes in 10.337 seconds at comparable wall time (`research/diagonal-residual-control-equalwall-review.md`). That is a single sample for a different family, not a measurement of this rule, but it is a strong reason to gate additional scans on measured benefit. Prellberg §5.5 likewise reports no consistent improvement from the specific guidance and warm starts tried there; neither observation predicts this cut's performance.

## Validation and compatibility

First prove the radius mapping and inequality independently. Exhaust small multigraphs, demands and parallel-edge cases against an independent edge-subset degree oracle, including the example above and feasible odd cycles. For tiny n3/n5/n7, compare complete solution sets and check every reachable partial state with an independent geometry/degree oracle. Require nonvacuous feasible cases and interruption tests showing no partial verdict. Separately measure hits and total predicate cost on matching small prefixes, retaining passing node order; reject the idea if it adds no meaningful pruning or costs more than it saves. A hit count alone is not evidence of speed.

The existing checkpoints bind source and policy and replay domains and counters. A new rejection changes logical search history even if the order of surviving batches stays identical. Preserve all frozen frontiers; any implementation and experimental run require separate review and a new source/policy identity and fresh genesis, unless a separately proved checkpoint migration is provided. This note proposes neither a migration nor an allocation.
