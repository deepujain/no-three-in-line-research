# Separate row-batch checkpoint chain

This separate chain adapts the approved single-orbit chain control policy to `diagonal-rowbatch-frontier-v1` and the row-batch guard. It does not modify or accept the old single-orbit frontier schema, and it does not change the existing chain, checkpoint cores, guards, helper or dispatcher. The standalone CLI uses `--seconds` for the aggregate ceiling (at most 900 seconds) and `--invocation-seconds` for each guard (at most 30 seconds); the RSS gate is sampled at at most 256 MiB. It reserves 32 seconds per launch, has at most 26 invocations, stops launching by the lesser of 840 seconds and aggregate ceiling minus 60 seconds, and retains a cleanup margin. These controls are cooperative/sampled, not kernel hard limits.

The runner requires a caller-supplied, externally trusted checkpoint digest and a new ledger directory. The source ledger binds its own code, row-batch guard and core, tiny reference, checkpoint utilities, original/fast cores, verifier, helper and current entrypoint. Before acceptance, it verifies the bounded checkpoint bytes, row-batch format, exact `(d,t)` selection and orbit/branch/source identity, parent digest, counters and per-slice monotonicity, guard cleanup/resource status and any saved witnesses. An unchanged checkpoint from a reconstruction timeout is rejected, not promoted as a new transition. Atomic ledger intent and completion records, a separate chain lock, and a sticky checkpoint owner marker prevent cooperating runners from automatically restarting interrupted or rejected chains. A hard kill or missing/rejected receipt requires a separate fresh process/ownership audit and explicit new allocation. Advisory locks do not protect against uncooperative writers, and a supplied expected hash is a trust anchor rather than authentication.

The chain stops after the first completed invocation with a verified witness, on timeout, exhaustion, failure or budget limit. The guard may process more nodes within that invocation after finding its first witness. A rejected post-publication checkpoint is tracked separately as published-but-unaccepted. The final receipt/marker writes and process exit have a cooperative tail; no hard wall deadline or power-loss durability guarantee follows.

The combined 61-test tiny suite passed, including row-batch witness-order prefix and no subsequent invocation, n=3/n=5 sliced worker runs, standalone CLI with mocked resource helper, source/identity/counter/resource faults, reservation and timeout stop, cancellation, interrupt and sticky-marker recovery, rejection of old schema and oversized checkpoint, and no-promotion handling of unchanged reconstruction timeout. This does not establish actual-host RSS or signal behavior. Independent source review, a separately coordinated dispatcher route, real-host tiny success and cancellation gates, and a separate allocation are required before n=79 chain use. No n=79 chain was run.
